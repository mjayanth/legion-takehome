The short version

Today's onboarding puts insurance, payment and scheduling ahead of the patient's need. The drop-off numbers are a symptom of that, and tweaking individual screens won't fix it. The measured flow keeps 23.8% of sessions to the payment page and loses most of the rest at two steps: describing care needs, and picking a clinician who is often fully booked.

    Friction points, hypotheses and A/B tests

    v2

    The umbrella hypothesis is that asking about the patient first lifts completion. Under it, 13 friction points become 13 hypotheses. 10 get an A/B test and 3 ship to everyone. At about 197 sessions a day, a 4-week test detects 3–14% relative lifts, so we test whole funnels first.

    The new funnel: PRD and prototype spec

    v3

    The talk-first funnel, written up. The PRD sets the goals, the patient-first order and the tests built into each step. v3 adds sign-up right after the fit checks and a consented insurance check before matching. The spec defines the agent loop, the 15 tools, the rules the model can't override, and the 19 scenarios the prototype is checked against.

  5. 4

    The prototype

    v2

    One path is built end to end: a Texas adult paying with insurance or self-pay, through the first completed visit. Other paths go as far as the handoff and say where the prototype stops. A care navigator that asks what's going on before anything else, then does the work with your OK: checks fit, signs you up with an email code, checks your coverage on one screen, recommends a clinician with reasons, holds a time, and stays with you through the first visit. Press R, or "How this works", to see the friction each screen fixes and the A/B test behind it. Synthetic data only.
